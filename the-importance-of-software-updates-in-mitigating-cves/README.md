## Introduction

### Scenario: The Cost of Ignoring Updates

Imagine a ransomware attack cripples your organization because a critical update was delayed.

**How could this have been prevented?**

- Regular review of CVEs.
- Installing the update that patches the vulnerability.

## The Risks of Delayed Updates

Take a moment to reflect on your experience with software updates. Think of the last time you received an alert prompting you to update your computer's operating system. Did you respond to it immediately or put it off for later? Why?

People and organizations often delay updating software for a variety of reasons, one of which is that certain updates cause interruptions to workflow or lengthy downtime.

Timely updates are crucial because they often include essential bug fixes and patches that address critical security vulnerabilities.

- Delayed updates leave systems vulnerable to attacks.
- Cybercriminals often exploit known CVEs quickly after disclosure; these are also known as ***"zero-day-exploits."***
- Unpatched vulnerabilities can escalate into more severe security breaches, exposing sensitive data or disrupting services.

### Real-World Example: `CVE-2017-0144`

The "WannaCry" ransomware attack (2017) exploited a Windows SMB (Server Message Block) vulnerability, causing billions of dollars in damage. Microsoft released a patch two months earlier, but many organizations delayed updating their systems. Although slightly nuanced, some of these organizations, especially those with 24/7 operations such as hospitals, concluded that the risk of disrupting essential services (***taking systems down to apply patches***) outweighed the perceived threat of an attack.

## Why Software Updates Matter

When you think of the term "Software Update," what comes to mind? Think of all the applications you have on your smartphone, tablet, or personal computer. How often do these get updated?

Software updates are important because they allow developers to patch vulnerabilities, enhance functionality, or improve performance.

Here are a few examples of the different types of updates:

- **Security Updates**: Address vulnerabilities identified through CVEs.
- **Feature Updates**: Add new functionality.
- **Bug Fixes**: Correct errors impacting usability or performance.

## Wrap-Up: Best Practices for Timely Software Updates

Now that we've discussed the importance of software updates and why they matter in mitigating CVEs, let's review some actionable best practices to ensure your systems stay updated.

- **Stay Informed**: Check for updates from software vendors regularly and use vulnerability feeds, such as the National Vulnerability Database (NVD), to stay ahead.
- **Automate When Possible**: Enable automatic updates for critical systems to apply patches promptly.
- **Test in Controlled Environments**: Test updates for enterprise systems in staging environments to prevent downtime.
- **Patch Management Policy**: Prioritize updates based on CVSS severity scores, and establish a process of identifying, testing, and deploying updates across all affected systems.
